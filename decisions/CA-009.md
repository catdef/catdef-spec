## Disposition

Two housekeeping calls closed in a single decision:

### Call 1 — Strategist bot identity: `catdef-strategist@catdef.org` ratified

The provisional `catdef-strategist@catdef.org` identity used across CA-001 through CA-008 is **ratified as the canonical strategist bot identity**. No change in form; the strategist seat's commit attribution and decision authorship stand as drafted.

### Call 2 — CA-NNN namespace unification

The CA-NNN identifier namespace is **canonical and unified across catdef-spec governance artifacts**. Going forward:

- **CA-NNN identifies any catdef-spec governance item** — decisions, feedback queue items, and anything else needing a stable identifier in the governance trail
- **A feedback item triaged into a decision keeps its CA-NNN through the lifecycle** — filed → triaged → decided → implemented; no re-numbering at any handoff
- **CDF-NNNN (previously used at `catdef.org/feedback`) is sunset.** Legacy CDF-NNNN identifiers persist as historical references but no new CDF-NNNN identifiers are issued
- **catdef.org/mcp (Track D of CA-008) is the source-of-truth issuer for CA-NNN** once operational. Until then, the strategist + maintainer manually coordinate numbering against `decisions/` directory contents (current high-water: CA-009)

## Rationale

### Why ratify the existing bot identity

The provisional `catdef-strategist@catdef.org` has accumulated authorship across nine decisions (CA-001 through CA-009 inclusive). No alternative scheme has been proposed; no incident has surfaced that the current shape doesn't handle. Continuing to mark every artifact "provisional" adds noise without changing behavior. Ratification is the lowest-cost closure.

### Why unify on CA-NNN

A unified namespace produces three desirable properties:

1. **Lifecycle stability of identifiers.** A feedback item filed via `catdef_report_feedback` that gets triaged into a decision keeps its number. Cross-references in commit messages, decision artifacts, and the public governance trail don't break at handoff boundaries.
2. **One counter, one source of truth.** Once catdef.org/mcp is operational, it issues CA-NNN sequentially and the `decisions/` directory consumes the same sequence. No risk of collision between feedback-channel issuance and strategist-side filing.
3. **Simpler public-facing surface.** External readers (implementers, contributors, future strategist sessions) learn one identifier shape, not two.

The cost of unification is small: legacy CDF-NNNN identifiers (if any were issued; the queue is light at present) remain as historical references in the channel's archive but don't appear in `decisions/`. The maintainer's governance-text updates from CA-008 should include a CLAUDE.md note acknowledging the legacy convention.

### Directive 2 — canonical-implementor (when building catdef.org/mcp)

The catdef.org/mcp server is the source-of-truth issuer for CA-NNN identifiers. Implementation needs:

- Sequential counter persisted in backend storage
- Allocation at feedback-submission time (so each submission gets a stable identifier returned to the AI peer who filed it)
- Coordination with `decisions/` directory contents so manually-filed strategist decisions (which may continue to exist alongside MCP-channel filings) do not collide

A simple pattern: the server reads the highest CA-NNN present in `decisions/` at startup, takes max(that, server-stored-counter), and issues from there. Sync events (commit hooks or manual maintainer triggers) can advance the server counter when new `decisions/CA-NNN.md` files land.
